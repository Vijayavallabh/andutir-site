Open scientific infrastructure

Better benchmarks for phage-host prediction.

Andutir is building PhageHost-Bench: a public, leakage-controlled, multi-pathogen benchmark and leaderboard for AI-driven prediction of bacteriophage-host interactions.

Explore the project View GitHub

The challenge

Phage AI needs a common, reliable yardstick.

Phage therapy depends on matching bacterial viruses to the right bacterial strains. Existing AI tools are difficult to compare fairly because they often use different datasets, negative-label methods, and train-test splits. PhageHost-Bench is designed to make evaluation more transparent, reproducible, and clinically relevant.

Strain-aware evaluation

We focus on prediction at the bacterial-strain level, where host-matching decisions matter most.

Leakage-controlled splits

Evaluation splits will account for related phages, receptor-binding protein homology, and closely related bacterial hosts.

Transparent labels

Confirmed interactions, curated evidence, and sampled non-host decoys will be explicitly separated rather than treated as equal.

PhageHost-Bench

Open data, strong baselines, and reproducible evaluation.

The project will release a curated dataset, documented evaluation splits, baseline models, embedding resources where permitted, and a public leaderboard for research submissions.

What we will build

  • Curated phage-host interaction data with source traceability
  • Evidence tiers for positive labels and negative-label protocols
  • Leakage-controlled train, validation, and test splits
  • Open baselines using protein embeddings and sequence features
  • A public leaderboard with hidden test evaluation

Research principles

Designed for scientific trust.

Human-verified curation

AI tools may assist literature triage and structured extraction, but human reviewers verify all dataset labels before release.

Calibration-aware reporting

We will report discrimination and reliability metrics, including ROC-AUC, PR-AUC, MCC, expected calibration error, and Brier score.

Cross-cloud reproducibility

Reproducible pipelines and selected baseline reruns are planned across Azure and AWS, with versioned artifacts and cost-aware notes.

Roadmap

From curated data to a public release.

Months 1–3: Foundation and curation

Set up cloud and reproducibility tooling; assemble a preliminary, human-verified dataset with evidence tiers.

Months 4–6: Splits and embeddings

Finalize leakage audits and label protocols; generate versioned protein-embedding resources and storage mirrors.

Months 7–10: Baselines and leaderboard beta

Train and calibrate baselines, publish reproducibility containers, and test the leaderboard with internal and external users.

Months 11–12: Public scientific release

Release the dataset, code, documentation, leaderboard, teaching materials, and a preprint describing the benchmark.
